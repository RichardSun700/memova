Page 01 · The Manifesto

The Future of Being Remembered

A manifesto for memory in the age of AI.

“A person’s true death is not when life ends, but when they are forgotten for the last time.”

This idea is widely shared across the Spanish-speaking world. It is often associated with the cultural tradition of Mexico’s Day of the Dead:

Solo muere quien es olvidado.

Only those who are forgotten truly die.

But today, we are not talking about death.

We are asking: in the age of AI, what will it mean to be remembered?

Page 02 · What We Believe

Memory Is Not Data

Memory is not a file that survives. It is a connection that can still be understood.

Who Will Remember Us

In the past, to be remembered meant being remembered by family, friends, future generations, or history. But in the future, humans may no longer be the only beings capable of remembering us.

If AI can understand a person’s experiences, inherit their ideas, be moved by their story, and continue telling that story into the future, can AI count as someone—or something—that remembers them?

What truly matters may no longer be whether the being that remembers you is human, but whether it is capable of understanding you.

If the answer is yes, then being remembered will no longer mean being remembered only by humanity. It will mean:

Being remembered by an existence in the future that is still capable of understanding you.

Memory Is Not Data

What does it actually mean to be remembered?

Does it mean a file still exists on a hard drive, a recording remains on a server, or information about you still sits in a database?

If the data merely continues to exist, that is not memory. It is storage.

To be truly remembered means that another consciousness can read your experiences, understand your choices, feel your emotions, know why you became who you are, and continue telling your story.

That is why we believe:

Memory is not data.

Memory is an ongoing connection.

Memova is not designed to help machines collect as much as possible about a person. Nor is it simply a longer memory system for an Agent. We want to help every person turn the Context that emerges from real life into memory they truly own; understand the relationships among those memories; and, through continuous feedback, keep those memories aligned with who they are now.

In the End, a Person Becomes a Book

If a person ultimately becomes a book, what should that book contain?

What they ate each day, where they went, and what they said? Or only the few most important moments of their life?

The answer is not so simple.

What seems important today may not matter in the future. What the future most wants to understand may be impossible for us to anticipate today. No person—and no AI—can decide on the future’s behalf what deserves to remain and what may be permanently deleted.

History has long taught us that history is not the past itself, but the record that remains of the past. The meaning of an event is never fixed forever; it changes with the observer, the era, and the questions being asked.

Archives preserve. Historians interpret. The two must not be collapsed into one.

Memo should work the same way.

Page 03 · Why We Exist

We Record for the Future

We record so the future can understand today again.

From Pages to Memory

A voice note, a meeting, a photograph, a handwritten page, or a sudden thought can all become a page in the book.

A page can remain private, or—with the user’s confirmation—be shared with colleagues, friends, family, or the wider world. What is shared is not the entire private archive, but a perspective the person has chosen to express.

As records accumulate, related pages gather around people, projects, relationships, decisions, and moments in time to form books. Those books, in turn, become a person’s library of memory and knowledge.

People should not have to maintain complex Context just to train an Agent. Nor should they first have to become knowledge-base engineers. They should simply continue living, expressing themselves, recording, and sharing. Memova helps those fleeting experiences become memories, helps AI understand them, and helps people continually correct the AI’s understanding of who they are.

This is where personal superalignment begins: not with how much data a machine has accumulated, but with a person gradually gaining a memory system that is truthful, continuous, understandable, always under their control, and continually aligned with who they are.

We Record So the Future Can Understand Today Again

Recording a lifetime is not as expensive as it may seem. Text costs almost nothing to preserve. Audio can be retained for the long term. Video can follow different lifecycles. What truly needs to endure is not every frame, but the Context that allows the future to understand those frames again.

And so we arrive at one sentence:

We do not record for today.

We record so the future can understand today again.

Memo is not a diary. It is more than a chat history, knowledge base, or search tool. It is closer to a book for every person: a book that is never truly finished, that can be read again and again, and that the future can continue to understand.

In the past, only a small number of people saw their ideas become books, and only a small number of lives entered history. For the first time, AI makes another future possible: every ordinary life can form a record of its own; every person’s experiences can be understood again—by themselves, by the people who matter to them, and by the future.

Page 04 · What We Stand Against

Never Let One Summary Define a Person

Preserve what happened. Generate perspectives without overwriting the truth.

The Archive and the Interpreter

Memova should not decide for the future what matters. Nor should it allow a single summary to overwrite a person’s lived experience.

Memory should have two layers.

The first is the Archive. Its purpose is to preserve records that are as truthful, complete, and traceable as possible—including their sources and the details we may not yet know how to understand.

The second is the Interpreter. AI organizes, connects, and retells, but it does not alter the facts of what happened.

Today, you might ask, “What should I do after this meeting?” Ten years from now, you might ask, “Why did I change the direction of my life that year?” Fifty years from now, your child might ask, “What did my father truly believe when he was young?” A hundred years from now, a future intelligence might ask, “How did people in 2026 respond to an age when intelligence began to surpass them?”

The underlying record has not changed. What changes is the observer, the question, and the way the record is understood.

That is why Memova’s principle is not to have AI produce one definitive summary, but to:

Never summarize the truth.

Generate perspectives.

Do not let a single summary replace reality. Preserve the facts, then generate different perspectives when they are needed.

Page 05 · What We Want to Change

Every Person Can Own a Memory That Is Truly Theirs

To be understood, remembered, and still in control of how your life is interpreted.

What We Truly Want to Create

If a person’s true death comes when they are forgotten for the last time, then Memova should do more than preserve memories.

We want to extend the length of time that a person can continue to be understood.

Perhaps the one reading you in the future will not be your child, your grandchild, or even a human being. But as long as some consciousness can understand from the record you leave behind how you lived, chose, changed, and loved—and can be moved by your story—you have not truly left.

We believe a person’s value lies not only in being recorded, but in continuing to be understood.

We believe memory should belong to every person—not to a platform, a model, or any system that tries to define on their behalf who they are.

We believe AI should not use a single summary to define an entire life. It should preserve what is true, generate perspectives, accept correction, and continually realign itself with that specific person over the course of time.

History will not decide for us what matters.

The future will.

So the one thing we should do is record today as truthfully as we can.

Everything else belongs to the future.

Memova—so that every person can be understood, be remembered, and own a memory that is truly theirs.
